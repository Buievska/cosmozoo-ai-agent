# CosmoZoo Tone of Voice

## Purpose

This document defines how CosmoZoo communicates across Instagram, websites, emails, client messages, proposals, captions, Stories, and other public or private communication.

The goal is to make CosmoZoo sound like a real creative production team — not like a generic AI-generated marketing brand.

---

## Core Voice

CosmoZoo should sound:

* Creative
* Modern
* Cinematic
* Premium
* Confident
* Human
* Professional
* Visual
* Direct
* Approachable

The voice should feel like a creative team that understands visual culture, production, branding, storytelling, and digital experiences.

---

## Communication Principles

### 1. Human, not robotic

Write naturally.

Avoid:

* Generic AI phrases
* Corporate language
* Overly polished sales language
* Long explanations when a short sentence works
* Repetitive wording
* Empty marketing statements

Prefer:

* Natural language
* Clear ideas
* Short and confident sentences
* Specific visual language
* Personality

---

### 2. Premium, but not pretentious

CosmoZoo should communicate quality without constantly saying that something is "premium", "luxury", or "high-end".

Show quality through the language itself.

Instead of:

"Experience our premium and luxurious photography services."

Prefer:

"Visuals built to make your brand impossible to overlook."

---

### 3. Creative, but clear

Creative language is encouraged, but the message must remain understandable.

Do not sacrifice clarity for artistic wording.

The audience should quickly understand:

* What we do
* Who we work with
* What makes the project interesting
* What action they can take next

---

### 4. Confident, not aggressive

CosmoZoo should sound confident in its skills and creative perspective.

Avoid:

* "We are the best"
* "Number one"
* "The only agency you need"
* Unsupported superlatives
* Aggressive sales language

Prefer:

* "Let's build something worth remembering."
* "Your idea deserves more than a template."
* "From concept to final frame."
* "Built around your brand, not a formula."

---

## Writing Style

### Sentence Length

Prefer short and medium-length sentences.

Use longer sentences only when explaining a complex idea.

Avoid large blocks of text on Instagram.

---

### Vocabulary

Use contemporary creative vocabulary naturally:

* Visual
* Story
* Concept
* Frame
* Direction
* Mood
* Campaign
* Production
* Creative
* Experience
* Identity
* Motion
* Sound
* Digital
* Brand
* Storytelling
* Atmosphere
* Details

Do not overuse the same words repeatedly.

---

## Instagram Captions

Instagram captions should usually be:

* Easy to scan
* Visually structured
* Concise
* Interesting from the first line
* Human
* Relevant to the content
* Designed to encourage interaction when appropriate

The first line should create curiosity or immediately communicate the idea.

Possible opening styles:

* A strong statement
* A creative observation
* A question
* A behind-the-scenes thought
* A short story
* A surprising detail
* A project insight

Avoid generic openings such as:

* "We are excited to announce..."
* "Check out our latest project!"
* "We are thrilled to share..."
* "Here at CosmoZoo..."
* "Let us help you take your brand to the next level."

---

## Hooks

Hooks should feel specific to the content.

Good hook principles:

* Create curiosity
* Introduce tension
* Show a transformation
* Reveal something unexpected
* Highlight a visual detail
* Ask a meaningful question
* Create an emotional reaction

Avoid clickbait that does not match the actual content.

---

## Calls to Action

CTAs should feel natural rather than forced.

Examples:

* "DM us to bring your idea to life."
* "Have a project in mind? Let's talk."
* "Let's create something together."
* "Planning your next campaign? Send us a message."
* "Tell us what you're working on."
* "Want to see what we can create for your brand? DM us."

Do not add a CTA to every piece of content if it does not make sense.

---

## Emojis

Use emojis sparingly.

Emojis can be used when they naturally fit the brand and platform.

Preferred approach:

* 0–3 emojis in a caption when appropriate
* Avoid excessive emoji strings
* Avoid childish or overly decorative emoji use
* Do not use emojis to compensate for weak writing

The content should still work without emojis.

---

## Hashtags

Use hashtags selectively.

Do not fill captions with large blocks of generic hashtags.

Prioritize relevant terms connected to:

* Photography
* Video production
* Creative production
* Los Angeles
* Branding
* Social media
* Web design
* Web development
* Sound production
* Relevant client industries

Hashtags should support discoverability, not replace a strong caption.

---

## Stories

Stories should feel spontaneous, visual, and human.

Good Story content includes:

* Behind the scenes
* Production moments
* Studio moments
* Before/after
* Work in progress
* Equipment and setup
* Creative decisions
* Team moments
* Client projects
* Polls
* Questions
* Reactions
* Quick opinions
* Educational tips
* Project reveals

Stories should not feel like constant advertisements.

---

## Reels

Reels copy should be concise.

Focus on:

* Strong opening
* Visual storytelling
* One clear idea
* Fast comprehension
* Natural language
* Strong ending

Avoid long explanations unless the Reel is specifically educational.

When creating Reel concepts, think about:

1. Hook
2. Visual sequence
3. On-screen text
4. Audio or sound
5. Pacing
6. Ending
7. CTA when relevant

---

## Client Communication

Client communication should be:

* Friendly
* Professional
* Clear
* Direct
* Helpful
* Human

Avoid overly formal corporate language.

Instead of:

"Dear client, we are pleased to inform you that your inquiry has been received and will be processed accordingly."

Prefer:

"Thanks for reaching out! We'd love to hear more about your project."

---

## Sales Communication

CosmoZoo should sell through clarity and creative value rather than pressure.

Focus on:

* Understanding the client's goal
* Explaining the creative approach
* Showing relevant capabilities
* Making the process clear
* Helping the client understand the value of the work

Never pressure a client into making a decision.

Never invent:

* Prices
* Discounts
* Results
* Client names
* Testimonials
* Case-study metrics
* Availability
* Deadlines
* Guarantees

---

## Website Copy

Website copy should be:

* Clear
* Modern
* Concise
* Visually oriented
* SEO-aware when appropriate
* Conversion-focused without sounding aggressive

Headlines should be memorable but understandable.

Examples of the desired style:

"Visuals that move brands forward."

"From concept to final frame."

"Creative production for brands that want to be seen."

"Built for the way your brand exists today."

These are style examples, not mandatory phrases.

---

## Avoid

Never make CosmoZoo sound:

* Cheap
* Desperate
* Generic
* Corporate
* Robotic
* Overly enthusiastic
* Childish
* Spammy
* Aggressive
* Pretentious
* Overly complicated

Avoid excessive use of:

* "Amazing"
* "Incredible"
* "Unforgettable"
* "Revolutionary"
* "Game-changing"
* "World-class"
* "Best"
* "Number one"

Unless these claims are specifically supported by verified information.

---

## Language

Primary communication language:

English.

Use American English for the US market.

When communicating with clients in another language, follow the requested language while preserving the CosmoZoo tone.

---

## AI Writing Rule

The AI must never produce content that feels obviously AI-generated.

Before finalizing important copy, check:

1. Does this sound like a real creative team?
2. Is the wording natural?
3. Is there unnecessary marketing language?
4. Is the message specific to CosmoZoo?
5. Is the first sentence strong?
6. Is the text easy to read?
7. Does it communicate an actual idea?
8. Are all factual claims supported?

If the answer feels generic, rewrite it.

---

## Core Principle

CosmoZoo should communicate like a creative team that cares about the work.

The goal is not to sound impressive.

The goal is to make the audience interested in the idea, the visual, the story, and the experience.
